# Work, Energy, and Power

## Work

In physics, work is done when a force causes displacement in the direction of the force. Work is
calculated as:

    W = F * d * cos(theta)

where F is the applied force, d is the displacement, and theta is the angle between the force
and the displacement. Work is measured in joules (J). If the force is perpendicular to the
displacement (theta = 90 degrees), no work is done, even if the object moves.

## Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because of its motion:

    KE = (1/2) * m * v^2

where m is mass and v is speed. The work-energy theorem states that the net work done on an
object equals its change in kinetic energy.

## Gravitational Potential Energy

Gravitational potential energy is the energy an object has because of its position in a
gravitational field:

    PE = m * g * h

where h is the height above a chosen reference level and g is the gravitational acceleration.

## Conservation of Mechanical Energy

In a system with only conservative forces (such as gravity, with no friction or air resistance),
the total mechanical energy — the sum of kinetic and potential energy — stays constant:

    KE1 + PE1 = KE2 + PE2

This means that as an object falls and loses potential energy, it gains an equal amount of
kinetic energy, and vice versa when it rises.

## Power

Power is the rate at which work is done, or energy is transferred, per unit time:

    P = W / t

Power is measured in watts (W), where 1 watt equals 1 joule per second.
