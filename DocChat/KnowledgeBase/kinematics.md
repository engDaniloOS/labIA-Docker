# Kinematics

Kinematics is the branch of physics that describes motion without considering its causes. It
studies quantities such as position, displacement, velocity, and acceleration, and how they
relate to time.

## Uniform Motion (constant velocity)

In uniform motion, velocity is constant and acceleration is zero. The position at time t is
given by the equation:

    s = s0 + v * t

where s0 is the initial position, v is the (constant) velocity, and t is the elapsed time. The
average speed over any interval equals the instantaneous speed at every point, since velocity
does not change.

## Uniformly Accelerated Motion

When acceleration is constant but not zero, three equations describe the motion:

    v = v0 + a * t
    s = s0 + v0 * t + (1/2) * a * t^2
    v^2 = v0^2 + 2 * a * (s - s0)

Here v0 is the initial velocity and a is the constant acceleration. If a has the same sign as
v0, the object speeds up; if a has the opposite sign, the object slows down.

## Free Fall

Free fall is a special case of uniformly accelerated motion where the acceleration is due to
gravity, approximately g = 9.8 m/s^2 near Earth's surface, directed downward, and air resistance
is ignored. An object dropped from rest reaches a speed of about 9.8 m/s after 1 second, about
19.6 m/s after 2 seconds, and so on, following v = g * t.
